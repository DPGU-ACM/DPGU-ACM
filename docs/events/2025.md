## September 2025: Innovative Idea Exhibition & ACM Club Inauguration

![Innovative Idea Exhibition Poster Presentation Hall](../../assets/events/innovative-idea-exhibition-gallery.jpg)
*Figure 1: Student participants presenting research posters during the inaugural AI exhibition.*

### Overview & Significance
- **Official Activity Title**: Innovative Idea Exhibition — Theme: Artificial Intelligence
- **Date**: September 2025 (Monday, 15th September 2025 — Engineer's Day)
- **Venue**: School of Technology & Research (STR), Dr. D. Y. Patil Dnyan Prasad University (DPGU), Pimpri, Pune
- **Organized By**: ACM Student Chapter, School of Technology & Research, DPGU
- **Foundational Milestone**: Marked the **Official Formal Inauguration of the ACM Club** under the DPGU ACM Student Chapter. Commemorated on Engineer's Day, the event established the chapter's formal charter to foster technical innovation, peer collaboration, and competitive computing within the university.

### Objectives
1. Provide a competitive academic platform for undergraduate engineering students to present creative solutions and prototypes in Artificial Intelligence.
2. Encourage research-driven thinking, interdisciplinary problem-solving, and peer knowledge exchange.
3. Establish active collaborative ties with prominent engineering institutions across the Pune technical ecosystem.
4. Inaugurate the ACM Club under the ACM Student Chapter as a dedicated technical society for student developers and researchers.

### Inter-Collegiate Participation
The exhibition drew technical poster submissions and student teams from five engineering institutions:
1. **Dr. D. Y. Patil Dnyan Prasad University (DPGU), Pimpri, Pune** *(Host Institution)*
2. **MIT Academy of Engineering (MIT AOE), Alandi, Pune**
3. **JSPM's Rajarshi Shahu College of Engineering (RSCOE), Tathawade, Pune**
4. **Marathwada Mitra Mandal College of Engineering (MMCOE), Pune**
5. **Pimpri Chinchwad College of Engineering (PCCOE), Akurdi, Pune**

### Thematic Focus & Technical Domains
Participants exhibited research posters, conceptual system architectures, and workflow diagrams across six core AI specializations:
- **Healthcare Diagnostics & Informatics**: AI-assisted early disease detection, clinical record parsing, and biomedical imaging.
- **Adaptive Education**: Intelligent tutoring agents and personalized learning analytics.
- **Sustainable Development**: Energy optimization algorithms and smart environmental monitoring.
- **Robotics & Kinematics**: Autonomous navigation and robotic manipulation pipelines.
- **Industrial Automation**: Computer vision for quality assurance and defect detection.
- **Smart City Systems**: Algorithmic traffic flow optimization and connected municipal telemetry.

### Industry Judging Panel
Posters and verbal defenses were rigorously evaluated by a panel of external industry leaders:
- **Mr. Sanjay Pawase** — Technical Consultant, **Atos Syntel**
- **Mr. Yash Patel** — Technical Lead, **HCLTech**
- **Ms. Preeti Chadchankar** — Senior Quality Analyst, **LTIMindtree**

*Evaluation Criteria*: Technical Innovation, Originality of Approach, Theme Relevance, Structural Clarity of Defense, and Practical Applicability.

## November 2025: Code Quest 2025

![Code Quest Programming Session](../../assets/events/code-quest-gallery.jpg)
*Figure 2: First-year engineering students competing during the live HackerRank coding round.*

### Overview
- **Official Activity Title**: Code Quest 2025
- **Dates**:
  - **Round 1 (MCQ Screening)**: 20th November 2025 (Conducted online via wayground.com)
  - **Round 2 (HackerRank Coding Arena)**: 22nd November 2025 (Saturday)
  - **Pre-Event Practice Quizzes**: Completed prior to 20th November 2025
- **Venue**: School of Technology & Research, DPGU, Pimpri, Pune
- **Organized By**: Problem Solving through Programming Team in Association with ACM Student Chapter, STR, DPGU

### Objectives
1. Rigorously assess syntax mastery, logical thinking, and computational accuracy in the C programming language.
2. Foster a culture of competitive programming early in students' undergraduate careers.
3. Bridge introductory classroom coursework with industry-standard competitive evaluation platforms (HackerRank).
4. Identify high-aptitude programmers for chapter competitive coding training cohorts.

### Competition Structure & Rounds
- **Round 1 — Foundational MCQ Screening (20 November 2025)**:
  - **Platform**: Hosted online on `wayground.com`.
  - **Target Audience**: Mandatory participation for **all First Year B.Tech (CSE) students across all 7 academic divisions (Divisions A through G)**.
  - **Syllabus**: Pointer arithmetic, memory layout, control structures, function recursion, bitwise operations, and standard library behaviors in C.
  - **Shortlist Threshold**: The **top 40 performers** across the cohort qualified for Round 2.
- **Round 2 — Final HackerRank Coding Challenge (22 November 2025)**:
  - **Platform**: Hosted on HackerRank (`hackerrank.com/code-quest-2025-1`).
  - **Format**: 5 complex algorithmic problem statements in C under strict time caps.
  - **Evaluation Criteria**: Test case correctness, boundary value handling, and execution time complexity (`O(n)` efficiency).

## December 2025: OOP Sprint Challenge

![OOP Sprint Simulation Session](../../assets/events/oop-sprint-gallery.jpg)
*Figure 3: 3-member teams developing C++ object-oriented simulation models.*

### Overview
- **Official Activity Title**: OOP Sprint Challenge: Code, Create, Compete ("Where Logic Meets Real World Design")
- **Date & Timing**: 1st December 2025 (Monday), 10:30 AM – 2:30 PM (4-Hour Intensive Sprint)
- **Venue**: Programming Laboratory 1 (PL Lab-I), STR Building, DPGU, Pune
- **Organized By**: Department of Computer Science and Engineering in Association with ACM Chapter DPGU

### Objectives
1. Reinforce practical application of core Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) paradigms: encapsulation, data abstraction, class inheritance hierarchies, and runtime polymorphism.
2. Train students to translate ambiguous real-world organizational requirements into modular, reusable C++ class architectures.
3. Develop rapid debugging, collaborative code integration, and technical defense capabilities under time-constrained sprint conditions.

### Sprint Format & Execution
- **Team Size**: Exactly 3 members per engineering sprint squad.
- **Task**: Each squad was assigned an enterprise operational scenario to model from scratch in C++.
- **Deliverables**:
  1. Clean, modular, well-commented C++ source code demonstrating class hierarchies and interfaces.
  2. Live interactive console simulation demonstrating state mutations and error handling.
  3. A 5-minute technical defense and live demonstration before faculty evaluators.
- **Evaluation Matrix**: OOP Class Architecture, Code Maintainability, Feature Completeness, Exception Handling, Creative Modeling, and Realism.

### Documented Real-World Simulation Statements
1. **Food Delivery Platform Simulation**: Multi-entity architecture modeling customers, restaurants, drivers, order status transitions, and dynamic pricing algorithms.
2. **Tea Stall Management System**: Micro-retail inventory ledger tracking raw ingredient consumption, sales billing, and profit margins.
3. **Grocery Store Billing Simulation**: Multi-category product SKU database with automated tax calculation, barcode simulation, and receipt generation.
4. **Car Wash Management System**: Service bay queuing model, booking scheduler, package selection, and technician assignment.
5. **Smart Campus Energy Balancer**: Multi-building power load distribution algorithm prioritizing critical educational infrastructure during peak demand.
6. **Ride Sharing System**: Geospatial coordinate simulation matching nearby drivers to passengers with variable fare calculation.
